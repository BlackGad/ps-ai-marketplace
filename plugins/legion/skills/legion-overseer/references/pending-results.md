# Pending results

The overseer who promises a future result owns it until the outcome is collected, acted on within the owner's authorization, and reported in the console and every connected channel. This applies to a background worker, hosted CI, an external job, a review, or any other result that may arrive later. A progress notice or a process exit is not the outcome. A failed check is not resolved by telling the owner it failed.

Before starting, pushing, or promising a result, record what is pending, where to obtain its result, and the exact `Next action` in the handoff. Use the existing `Legion running` row for a worker and the relevant stage and `Next action` for CI or another job; do not invent a separate tracker. Include the branch or run identifier and the expected evidence, without secrets. Keep the lease while overseeing. Start long work in the background where supported. If the tool provides a completion event, wait for it and verify the exit status. Otherwise, tell the owner in the console and every connected channel that the turn will stay open while waiting; use bounded status waits on the process or job, reassess after each wait, and repeat until a terminal result or genuine blocker. For hosted CI, identify the run after the push and follow it through a terminal result. Do not end an active turn while a promised result is pending, leave it to an owner status request, or repeatedly poll a log.

When the result arrives, read the worker report, job output, or hosted check details and verify its exit status and evidence. For a worker, follow the task review and indexed gate before clearing its row. For failed CI or an external job, investigate the cause, scope and dispatch a fix within existing authority, then watch the replacement run; do not merely report failure or rerun a flaky check to green. Record each transition and next action in the handoff before starting the next step. In Shared mode, commit CI watch and result transitions locally without pushing them on their own; push only with the next authorized code change, then watch the new run. This avoids a CI run for each status update. Report the resolved result on every connected channel, regardless of where the owner last wrote.

On restart, takeover, or handoff, reconcile every running row and every pending result named in the handoff before starting new work. For CI or an external job, query its recorded run identifier or branch, then collect, act on and report a terminal result; if still running, resume the active wait. A new lease holder inherits the follow-up; the prior holder transfers an accurate handoff before releasing the lease. Only when the session itself is ending, leave the pending result and retrieval action durable in the handoff and tell the owner that automatic observation stops when the session stops. Resume from that record on the next overseer start.

Pause only for a real owner decision or approval, a technical blocker, or an authorization boundary. Record the exact decision or blocker and the next action, ask through the shared channel rule, and keep ownership of the follow-up after the answer. Do not merge, deploy, install, spend, or expand scope without the required authority. A legion member continues to report to the overseer and never reports directly to the owner or clears the overseer's pending result.
